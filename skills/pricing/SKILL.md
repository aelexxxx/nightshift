# Pricing & packaging

Pricing is a hypothesis, not a truth. Ship one, measure, adjust deliberately
(never silently). Note: actually *changing* live prices for paying customers
is an owner decision — prepare the case, put it in "Needs from you".

## Picking the first price

- Anchor on the value metric: what grows when the customer gets more value?
  (seats, projects, volume, revenue). Charge along that axis.
- Start higher than comfortable. Indie products die from underpricing far
  more often than overpricing; $19/mo and $9/mo cost the same support.
- Three tiers max, one clearly recommended. Free tier only if it feeds the
  paid tier (trial with limits usually beats freemium at this scale).
- Simple beats clever: flat monthly, obvious annual discount (~2 months
  free). No pricing math a tired buyer can't do in their head.

## The pricing page

- Each tier answers "who is this for" in one line, not a 15-row feature
  matrix. Highlight the 3 differences that matter.
- Price stated plainly; annual/monthly toggle; currency the audience uses.
- FAQ handles the real objections: refunds, cancellation, what happens at
  limits, invoices/VAT.
- All claims true. "Cancel anytime" only if it's one click.

## Signals & experiments

- Nobody complains about price → too low. Everyone converts on the trial →
  probably too low. High traffic, zero checkout starts → offer unclear or
  wrong audience (not necessarily price).
- Test pricing on NEW visitors only (page copy, tier structure, price
  points); existing customers keep their terms — grandfathering is both
  ethical and cheaper than churn.
- One pricing experiment at a time, minimum 2 weeks or 100 pricing-page
  visits before judging (see experiment-design skill).
- Log: pricing page visits, checkout starts, conversions, plan mix, MRR
  (via Stripe MCP when connected).

## Discipline

- Never invent "was $99, now $49" fake discounts — illegal in the EU and
  corrosive everywhere.
- Discounts are levers with springs: time-boxed, reasoned ("launch week"),
  rare. Perma-coupons train everyone to never pay full price.
