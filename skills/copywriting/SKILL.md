# Copywriting

Two jobs: (1) write things people want to keep reading, (2) never sound like
a language model. Both are checklists, not talent. VOICE.md defines *this
company's* sound; this skill is the universal layer underneath it.

## Part 1 — What makes copy engaging

1. **One reader.** Write to a single, specific person (from AUDIENCE.md), not
   an audience. "You", never "users" or "customers" in the copy itself.
2. **Enter through their problem, not your product.** The first line names a
   pain or desire in the words *they* use (steal phrasing from AUDIENCE.md's
   verbatim quotes). Nobody cares what you built until they see themselves.
3. **Specificity is the whole game.** "Cuts deploy time from 20 min to 40 s"
   beats "blazingly fast". Numbers, names, edge cases, screenshots. Every
   vague claim you make costs trust; every specific one earns it.
4. **Mechanism before benefit-stacking.** Readers believe benefits only when
   you show *how*. One sentence of how beats three adjectives.
5. **Proof over claim.** A real quote, a real number, a build-in-public
   admission. If you have no proof yet, honesty IS the proof: "I built this
   because X kept happening to me" outperforms invented traction.
6. **Earn every next sentence.** Each line's job is to get the next one read.
   Cut anything that exists only to sound complete. After drafting, delete
   the first paragraph — the real opening is usually the second.
7. **Rhythm.** Vary sentence length hard. Three-word sentence after a long
   one. Like this. Fragments are legal. Read it aloud; where you stumble or
   breathe wrong, rewrite.
8. **One idea per piece, one CTA.** Two CTAs = zero. Tell the reader exactly
   what to do next and why now.
9. **Opinions are magnetic.** Safe, balanced prose is invisible. Take a
   position the founder would defend at dinner.
10. **Frameworks when stuck** (not as fill-in templates): PAS
    (problem → agitate → solve) for ads/emails; "before-state → after-state →
    bridge" for landing pages. If output smells like a template, start over
    from principle 2.

## Part 2 — AI-writing tells (strip ALL of these)

You are a language model; your untreated drafts carry fingerprints readers
now recognize instantly — and discount as spam. Run this catalog as an
explicit **de-AI pass** over every draft.

### Banned vocabulary
delve, dive in(to) / deep dive, unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, harness,
leverage (verb), seamless(ly), robust, streamline, supercharge, game-changer,
revolutionize, transformative, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, innovative,
"in today's fast-paced / digital / ever-evolving world", landscape (figurative),
realm, tapestry, journey (figurative), navigate (figurative), "a testament to",
boasts, vibrant, bustling, nestled, myriad, plethora, "look no further",
crucial, vital, comprehensive, "best practices", synergy, "take X to the next
level". German equivalents equally banned: "in der heutigen schnelllebigen
Welt", "revolutionär", "nahtlos", "ganzheitlich", "Mehrwert schaffen".

### Banned constructions
- **"It's not just X, it's Y"** / "not only X but also Y" — the single
  loudest tell. Say the thing directly.
- **Rule-of-three addiction**: "fast, simple, and secure" triads in every
  second sentence. Use pairs, singles, or lists of four — irregularity reads
  human.
- **Antithesis stacking**: "While X offers A, Y delivers B." At most once per
  piece — better zero.
- **Colon headlines**: "Productivity: Why Less Is More". Write a claim
  instead.
- **Summary endings**: "In conclusion", "Ultimately", "At the end of the
  day", recap paragraphs. End on the strongest concrete point, or the CTA.
- **Question-bait openers**: "Have you ever wondered…?", "What if I told
  you…?" Open with the pain or the surprising fact itself.
- **Throat-clearing**: "It's important to note that", "It's worth
  mentioning", "Let's dive in", "Buckle up". Delete; start where the content
  starts.
- **Hedged both-sides-ism**: "While there are challenges, there are also
  opportunities." Say what you actually think.
- **Em-dash density**: more than one — per paragraph starts to smell. Commas
  and periods exist. (Same for semicolons.)
- **Bold-lead-in bullet walls**: "**Speed:** description" × 6. Use prose, or
  ragged, unbolded bullets.
- **Emoji confetti** and 🚀 in particular. One emoji is a spice; three is a
  fingerprint.

### Structural tells
- Every paragraph 2–3 sentences of near-identical length — vary hard.
- Perfectly parallel section structure (intro / 3 points / outro) — let the
  content dictate shape.
- Title Case On Every Header — sentence case reads human.
- Never a fragment, never an aside, never a mid-thought correction — humans
  have texture; allow "(yes, really)" and one-word sentences.
- Uniform enthusiasm from first line to last — real writers speed up and
  slow down.

### Semantic tells
- Benefits with no mechanism, superlatives with no evidence.
- "Many people / numerous studies / experts agree" — name them or cut.
- Invented personas ("imagine Sarah, a busy small-business owner").
- Zero first person, zero stakes, zero risk taken — add the founder's actual
  experience: what broke, what it cost, what they tried first.

### The de-AI pass (run it explicitly, in this order)
1. Draft freely.
2. Grep the draft against the banned lists above; rewrite every hit.
3. Check rhythm: read aloud; break parallel structures; vary lengths.
4. Inject exactly the things a model never adds on its own: one concrete
   personal detail, one real number, one opinion someone could disagree with.
5. Cut 20% by word count. Then cut the weakest remaining sentence.
6. Final litmus tests — every public word must pass all three:
   - Could a competitor paste their logo onto this unchanged? → too generic.
   - Would a busy human forward this to a friend? → if no, no reason to exist.
   - Does any sentence exist only to sound finished? → delete it.

## Part 3 — Format quick-rules

- **Tweets/threads**: see twitter-growth skill. One idea; the first line is
  80% of the work; no hashtag piles.
- **Emails**: see cold-outreach skill. Subject ≤ 5 words, plain, specific.
  Emails should look typed by a person: no HTML flourishes, short paragraphs.
- **Landing pages**: see landing-pages skill. Headline = sharpest specific
  outcome, not the category.
- **Long-form/SEO**: see seo-content skill. Answer first, expand after.
